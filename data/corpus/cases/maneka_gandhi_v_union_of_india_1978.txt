MANEKA GANDHI V. UNION OF INDIA
Supreme Court of India, 1978

In Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India (1978), a seven-judge bench of the Supreme Court of India revolutionized the interpretation of Article 21 of the Constitution. The case arose when the passport of Maneka Gandhi was impounded by the government under the Passport Act, 1967, without giving any reasons.

The Supreme Court held that the phrase "procedure established by law" in Article 21 cannot be interpreted to mean any procedure, no matter how arbitrary or oppressive. Instead, the procedure depriving a person of life or personal liberty must be "fair, just and reasonable" and not "arbitrary, fanciful or oppressive." 

This interpretation effectively imported the American doctrine of "due process of law" into Indian constitutional jurisprudence. The Court ruled that Articles 14, 19, and 21 are not mutually exclusive water-tight compartments but form a "golden triangle," meaning any law depriving personal liberty under Article 21 must also satisfy the tests of equality under Article 14 and freedoms under Article 19.
