GRANBERRY V. COVAS (1999)
Supreme Court of California
Docket No. S076384. Decided September 23, 1999.

SUMMARY:
Tenants brought an action against their landlord to recover security deposits. The trial court entered judgment in favor of the tenants, finding that the landlord had failed to provide the itemized statement and refund required by California Civil Code Section 1950.5 within the mandatory 21-day period. The Court of Appeal affirmed.

OPINION BY JUSTICE MOSK:
In this case, we address the consequences that follow when a residential landlord fails to comply with the itemized statement and return requirements of Civil Code Section 1950.5. Specifically, we must determine whether a landlord who fails to provide the required itemized statement within 21 days loses all rights to retain any portion of the tenant's security deposit.

We conclude that a landlord who fails to comply with the 21-day requirement of Section 1950.5, subdivision (g)(1), forfeits the right to retain any portion of the security deposit. The statutory language is mandatory: the landlord "shall" furnish the itemized statement and return the remaining portion within 21 days. The prompt return of security deposits is vital to prevent abuse and ensure tenants have access to their funds to secure subsequent housing.

However, we also clarify that the landlord's forfeiture of the right to *retain* the deposit does not extinguish the landlord's underlying right to recover damages for unpaid rent, cleaning, or repairs. The landlord remains free to bring an independent action against the tenant for such damages, or to raise them as a cross-complaint if sued by the tenant. The forfeiture is limited to the right of self-help (retaining the funds without a court order). Thus, while the landlord must return the entire deposit immediately due to non-compliance with the 21-day timeline, they may still seek to recover damages in a court of law.
